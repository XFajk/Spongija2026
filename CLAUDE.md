# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## Project

Spongija2026 is a 3D turn-based strategy game based on Heroes of Might and Magic III (mechanics are added to or cut from it), with inspiration from HoMM IV and V (free mouse cursor, clickable 3D objects). It is built with **Godot 4.7.2 (.NET / C#)**. It targets .NET 8 (`Godot.NET.Sdk/4.7.2`, pinned in `global.json`) with nullable reference types enabled. All gameplay code is C# in `src/`; scenes live in `scenes/`; art/media in `assets/`. There are no tests and no linter configured.

Two parts exist: the adventure map (`scenes/map.tscn`, run with F6) and battles (`scenes/battle_test.tscn`). They're connected one way: a fight point of interest on the map starts a battle with the inventory's army (`BattleStarter`), but nothing returns to the map when the battle ends. The main scene doesn't lead to either.

## Game design

Agreed design. Values without a number yet are TBD.

### Setting and scope

- LOTR, Years of the Trees: the Elves' Great Journey from Cuiviénen toward Aman.
- The game covers only the Great Forest (later Mirkwood), ending at Atyamar in the Anduin Vale. A second part (crossing the Anduin and the Misty Mountains) is optional, only if time allows.
- The player is Oromë (the Vala). The story starts at the Elves' settlement in front of the forest, where they waited after being slaughtered by dark creatures in the forest, until Oromë came.
- A tutorial in front of the forest teaches movement, combat, artifacts and camps.

### Adventure map

- One large 3D map. The hero is a `NavigationAgent3D`: click a point and he walks there along the navmesh.
- No days, weeks, seasons or movement points (movement is unlimited). No towns.
- Areas (east to west): the start, Eastern Woods, the Meadow (centre hub, camp, wild horses), then two routes: the Deep Thicket (north, spiders) or Shadow Hill (south, shadows and spirits, ruins of a Melkor stronghold, optional boss that drops the Valaróma artifact). The Mere/swamp has ore, a mana spring and the strongest scroll. The end is Atyamar (west). The map sketch is not in the repo; ask the user for it.
- Scale: 1 Godot unit = 1 m. Hero ~2.5 m, trees 5–8 m, mountains 15–35 m, camp 15–20 m, paths at least 6–10 m wide. Camera ~35 m up, pitched 50–55°. Sizes follow gameplay importance, not realism.

### Army

- Starting army: ~30 pilgrim elves and 5 warrior elves.
- 5 army slots with no stack cap. Stacks can be split across slots as in HoMM: an attack hits only one stack, so splitting limits losses but uses slots.
- Ways to get units:
  1. Camps: on every hero move, each camp measures the navmesh path distance to the hero and stores the maximum ever reached (`max_ever`). A visit pays out only new progress: `max_ever - already_paid` is converted to units (X distance = 1 pilgrim, Y pilgrims = 1 warrior; X and Y TBD), then `already_paid = max_ever`. This prevents farming by walking back and forth.
  2. Found on the map (e.g. lost elves).
  3. Surrender: if the player's army is much stronger, the fight can be skipped and beasts (wolves, spiders) offer to join; the player accepts or refuses. Demons and shadows never surrender.
  4. Crafting with found resources: horses + pilgrims → cavalry, silk + pilgrims → archers, ore + pilgrims → warriors (ratios TBD).

### Hero, artifacts, spells

- As in HoMM3, the hero doesn't fight. His stats (Attack, Defense, Power, Knowledge) boost the army, and Knowledge sets max mana.
- 3 artifact slots: neck, left hand, right hand. Artifacts boost hero stats. One artifact per significant forest area (~6).
- Mana works as in HoMM3. Hero mana refills at mana springs, which work like camps. Unit mana refills on its own.
- Spells are learned in exactly 2 ways:
  1. From an enemy caster unit that surrenders and joins: the hero learns its spell, castable on that unit's turn or as a hero spell. The hero keeps the spell even if the unit dies later.
  2. From scrolls with a Knowledge requirement. A spell is usable only while current Knowledge >= its requirement; recheck whenever artifacts change. Strong spells need artifact-boosted Knowledge, and removing the artifact locks the spell again.
- Combat: HoMM3-style turn-based battles with stacks (details TBD).

### Built so far versus the design

- Camps (`RecruitPOI`), resources and crafting, army slots with splitting and swapping, and artifact equipping exist on the map (see Adventure map below). Artifacts don't change hero stats yet, and their slots are `head`/`body`, not neck and hands. The hero on the map has no stats: `BattleStarter` gives every battle a fixed hero.
- Not built: surrender, lost elves, scrolls, learning spells from units, mana springs.

## Branches & CI/CD

- `main` is the development branch. `stable` and `playtest` are release branches.
- `.github/workflows/itch-deploy.yml`: pushing to `stable` or `playtest` exports all three platforms and pushes them to itch.io via butler. Channels are `<platform>-<branch>`, e.g. `windows-playtest`. PRs into those branches only build, as a required check, and never deploy.
- The macOS build is exported on Linux and then re-signed ad-hoc with `codesign` on a macOS runner, followed by a headless smoke-launch. Godot's Linux-side signer produces a signature that macOS kills at launch, so don't remove this step.
- The Godot version is pinned in three places that must stay in sync: `GODOT_VERSION` in the workflow, `Spongija2026.csproj`, and `global.json`.

## Architecture

**Scene flow:** `scenes/starting_scene.tscn` is the main scene. It plays an intro video via `SplashScreen` (a `VideoStreamPlayer`), which on `Finished` switches to its exported `BeginningScene` (currently `scenes/test.tscn`, a script-less greybox of boxes). `scenes/main_menu.tscn` (`MainMenu`: title, settings and quit screens) isn't in the flow yet, and its Play button does nothing.

**Settings menu** (`src/ui/SettingsBuilder.cs`, in `main_menu.tscn`): builds the Video/Input/Audio/Mysc tabs from `SettingsPageResource`s (`assets/settings/*.tres`), each a list of `Setting` resources (`src/resources/`: `ToggleSetting`, `SliderSetting`, `Dropdown`, `TitleSetting`). Each setting instantiates its `SceneTemplate`, a control from `src/ui/Settings/`. The settings aren't applied or saved anywhere yet.

**Player and interaction** (`src/player/`, `src/interaction_system/`):

- `PlayerCamera` (`Camera3D`) casts a ray from the mouse every frame against collision layer 4 (areas included). A hit that implements `IInteractable` (`Interact`, `Focus`, `FocusLost`) gets focus; `Focus`/`FocusLost` are sent only when the focus changes. A click starts an interaction, which continues while `interact` (left mouse) is held and the ray stays on the object; the object loses focus meanwhile. `ArenaCamera` is the arena's subclass.
- `Interactable` (a `Node` child of the object, which forwards its `IInteractable` calls to it) turns the calls into signals: `InteractionStarted`, then `Interacting`/`PhysicsInteracting` every frame, and `InteractionFinished` on the first frame without an `Interact` call. Don't add members to `IInteractable` for this; put them on `Interactable`.
- `InteractionLock` keeps a running interaction going (signals still every frame) even when the ray misses or the button is released, until it's lifted. Meanwhile the static `Interactable.LockedInteractable` is set and the camera interacts with nothing else, but focus still follows the mouse unless `FocusLock` is set too. `InteractionExitFlag` ends an interaction right away.
- Hidden nodes still collide, so the ray would still hit them: tiles use `Enabled` (visibility and collision shape together) instead of `Show`/`Hide`.
- `PlayerCamera._interactionRayCast` is `protected`, so subclasses (`AdventureCamera`) can read the ray's hit.
- The camera polls `interact` instead of reading input events, so UI doesn't consume its clicks by itself. Two guards: while the mouse is over a UI control (`GetTree().Root.GuiGetHoveredControl()`, except the `ViewportRect` showing the view; controls that ignore the mouse don't count), nothing in the world is hovered and the ray is cleared; and UI that uses a click in `_Input` calls `PlayerCamera.IgnoreCurrentClick()`, so that click starts no interaction until the button is released (the spellbook does this when a click outside it closes it).

**Adventure map** (`scenes/map.tscn`, `src/player/`, `src/arena/*POI.cs`, the map UI in `src/ui/`; written by teammates; greybox ground, a wall and one point of interest of each kind). Most of these scripts look nodes up by absolute `/root/Map/...` paths, so they work only in `map.tscn` as its root.

- `AdventureCharacter` (`CharacterBody3D`, `scenes/player/adventure_character.tscn`) walks with a `NavigationAgent3D` at `Speed`, turning toward its path. `SetTarget(pos)` walks to a point and stops within `stopDistance`; `SetPOITarget(pos, distance, poi)` (ignored unless `canMove`) walks to a point of interest and stops within its `stopDistance`, then calls `poi.Access()` once it's there and not yet `accessed`. A new target unfocuses the previous point of interest. `moving` is true while it walks; `ForceStop` drops the path. Its children `Inventory` and `BattleStarter` (below) hold the army and start fights.
- Path preview: on each new target it gets the path from `NavigationServer3D.MapGetPath` and drops a `point` scene (`scenes/arena/path_point.tscn`) every `pointsMinDistance` into `container`, and a `finalPoint` (`scenes/arena/marker.tscn`, a red marker) at the end. Points within 0.5 m of the character are freed as it walks.
- `AdventureCamera` (`PlayerCamera` subclass, in a SubViewport shown by a `ViewportRect`, like the arena; `Agent` is the character) follows the character from `Distance` behind and `Height` up, smoothed by `SmoothSpeed`. Right drag orbits (yaw), middle drag pans (both only while `Agent.canMove`), and `jump` (Space) eases the pan back to the character; `returnPanningButton`, a label saying so, shows while panned. While panned, a `RayCast3D` (`/root/Map/SubViewport/RayCast3D`, layers 3 and 4) cast down from above the camera keeps the orbit center at ground height. A left click (`interact`) on collision layer 4 (bit `1 << 2`, the `Ground` areas) moves the character there if `Agent.canMove`, the point is on the navmesh and a path reaches it. Map UI that uses a click in `_Input` calls both `IgnoreAdventureCurrentClick()` (this camera's own flag, as its click check doesn't go through `PlayerCamera`'s) and `IgnoreCurrentClick()`.
- `PointOfInterest` (`Area3D`, `IInteractable`, layer 1 and 4): focus shows its `Highlight` mesh, a click walks the character to it and keeps the highlight until it's reached (`Access`, virtual, sets `accessed`) or another target is picked (`UnFocus`). `cursorIcon` isn't used yet. It finds the character at `/root/Map/AdventureCharacter` (`_character`, protected). Subclasses override `Access` and clear `_character.poi` when done:
  - `ResourcePOI`: adds `Amount` of its `TypeOfResource` (`horses`, `silk`, `iron`) to the inventory, shows a `FloatingText` (`scenes/ui/floating_text.tscn`: a label that rises and fades out, then frees itself) with the resource's icon, and frees itself.
  - `ArtefactPOI`: adds its `Artefact` to the inventory, shows a floating text, frees itself.
  - `RecruitPOI` (the camp, `CampPOI` in the map): the camp design above. Every frame it measures the navmesh path length to the character; the first visit gives 15 m worth, later visits only the distance beyond the farthest point reached since the last payout: one `pilgrimUnit` per `metersForPilgrim` and, once past `metersForWarrior`, also one `warriorUnit` per `metersForWarrior`. The units go to the inventory after one floating text per stack, while the character can't move.
  - `FightPOI`: when the character comes within `detectionDistance` (unless it's walking to this point already) or reaches it, it stops the character, hands `ComputerUnits`, `ComputerSpells` and `ComputerStrategy` to the `BattleStarter`, and opens the fight panel (`CharacterPanel.GetReady`: the `SetupFight` panel, tree paused). Its Fight button calls `AdventureCharacter.EnterFight`.
- `BattleStarter` (`src/battle_system/BattleStarter.cs`, a child of the character): `EnterBattle(spells, units)` builds the player's `Army` from the inventory's `Units` and `Spells`, with a fixed hero (Strength 2, Defense 2, Knowledge 5, Mana 30), and the computer's from what the `FightPOI` set (with the same hero if it has spells), then `SceneTransitions.EnterArena`. Nothing comes back from the battle: losses aren't written back to the inventory and the map isn't reloaded.
- `Inventory` (`src/player/Inventory.cs`, a `Node` child of the character): the army as `Units` (a fixed array of `StackResource` slots, null when empty; 5 in the scene), the hero's `Spells`, found `Artefacts`, `EquippedArtefacts`, resources (`horses`, `silk`, `iron`) and crafting `recipes` (`CraftingUnit` resources in `assets/battle_system/crafting/`: `Unit` + `Resource` → `FinalUnit`). Added units join a slot of the same unit, else an empty one; if none is free, they go to `unitsToSwap` and the swap window opens. It does splitting (move part of a stack to an empty slot or one of the same unit), combining (click a resource, then a stack: as many units as there are resources become the recipe's unit, needing a free slot or one of that unit), swapping and equipping, and calls `UpdateUI` to redraw the panel. `Unit.texture` is the unit's icon in the slots.
- `Artefact` (`src/battle_system/artefacts/`, `[GlobalClass]` Resource, `.tres` files in `assets/battle_system/artefacts/`): `Name`, `BodyPart` (`head` or `body`), `Icon`. No stats yet.
- Clicking the character (`AdventureCharacter.Interact`) opens the Character panel (`CanvasLayer/Canvas/Character`, `CharacterPanel`) and sets `canMove` false; Close or a click outside it (`_Input`) hides it (`HidePanel` also cancels splitting, combining and equipping). Its Inventory and Magic tabs slide by tweening their Y to `inventoryYPos`/`magicYPos`. Inventory: unit slots (`InventoryUnitSlot`, `scenes/ui/unit_slot.tscn`), resource buttons, the split window (`CanvasLayer/Canvas/Split`, a slider) and the artifacts (`ArtefactSlot`, `scenes/ui/artefact_ui.tscn`, equipped slots and a grid of found ones). Magic: a Spells button that opens `SpellDisplay` (`CanvasLayer/Canvas/Spells`), the battle spellbook's look and paging (`SpellButton`s, mana bar) showing the inventory's spells. `SwapPanel` (`SwapUnitSlot`, `scenes/ui/swap_slot.tscn`) lets the player pick which stacks to keep when the slots are full, with the tree paused.
- `PopoutManager` (`CanvasLayer/Canvas/PopoutContainer`): `SpawnPopout(title, text)` stacks a `scenes/ui/popout.tscn` and pauses the tree; each button closes the top one, and the tree unpauses after the last.
- `Tutorial` (`src/ui/Tutorial.cs`, the `Tutorial` node): popouts shown once each, tracked in the static `TutorialChecklist`: camera and movement 0.5 s after the map loads, the inventory hint after the first walk, the inventory explanation when the panel first opens, swapping when the swap window first opens.
- UI style: `assets/ui_themes/fantasy.tres` (Cinzel Decorative font, `assets/pics/scroll.png` panels), `HighlightLabelSettings.tres`, `unit_slot.tres`, `artefact_slot.tres`.

**Terrain** (`scenes/environment.tscn`, not used by `map.tscn` yet): the real forest, made with the Terrain3D addon (`addons/terrain_3d`, enabled in `project.godot`; its data and textures in `assets/terrain/`), with water, the starting camp and props (camp, Cernota ruins in `scenes/decorations/cernota/`, spider nest, swamp mine) and a baked navmesh. `src/environment/` holds GDScript tools: `Scatter.gd` (`@tool`, scatters `MultiMeshInstance3D` LODs and spawns collisions near the player, `RangedCollisions.gd` freeing them again) and `debug_freecam.gd`. `demo/` is the Terrain3D addon's demo, not part of the game.

**Spellbook** (`ArenaUserInterface`, `BookInterface` in `scenes/ui/arena_user_interface.tscn`): Arena calls `UpdateSpellButtons(stack)` at the start of a player turn: Use Spell is enabled only if the stack has spells of its own, Use Hero's Spell only if its hero knows any (`GetKnownSpells`); `UpdateSpellButtons(null)` in `ClearTurnInput` disables both between player turns. The battle interface's buttons are `TextureButton`s; disable them through `ArenaUserInterface.SetButtonDisabled`, which also fades them (and their labels) to `DisabledButtonAlpha` (0.25). They hide the battle interface and open the book with the current stack's `Unit.Spells` (disabled without the mana) or the hero's `GetKnownSpells()` (disabled unless `Hero.CanCast`). `BookInterface/ProgressBar` (theme `assets/ui_themes/mana_bar_theme.tres`: the rim texture as background, a pure blue fill) shows the caster's mana (the hero's, or the stack's for its own spells), with `ProgressBar/ManaLable` on it as `Mana: current/max`, both set when the book opens. Its material, `assets/ui_themes/mana_bar_material.tres`, runs `src/shaders/mana_bar.gdshader`: on pixels of `fill_key_color` (the fill's blue) only, two scrolling layers of `noise_texture` (default `assets/ui/mana_bar_noise.tres`, a seamless `NoiseTexture2D`) blend `deep_color` to `bright_color` with sparks; the rim is untouched. Changing the fill color in the theme means changing `fill_key_color` too. The `SpellButton`s (`src/ui/SpellButton.cs`, on `scenes/ui/spell_button.tscn`, 240 px; one per row, two rows per page, so 4 per spread; setting `Spell` shows its `Icon` and `Name`, and `SpellAbility.HoveredIcon` while the mouse is over an enabled button, tracked by the mouse signals; `FillSpellButtons` calls `UpdateIcon` after setting `Disabled`) are collected in reading order; a spread shows as many spells as there are buttons in the book, and buttons past the last spell are hidden. The `SpellBook` `AnimatedSprite2D` plays `open` (frames 0 to 47), Next plays `flip_page` forward, Back plays it backwards; the buttons are filled before and shown after each animation, Next only if more spells follow, Back only after the first spread. Input to the book is ignored while it animates. A click off the book closes it (in `_Input`, so it never reaches the battle). Picking a spell closes the book and emits `ArenaUserInterface.SpellPicked(spell, isHeroSpell)`, which starts `Arena.BeginSpellTargeting`: the normal turn input is cleared (`ClearTurnTargets`) and turned off (`IsPickingSpellTarget`), the tiles under the `GetSpellTargets` stacks show (red for enemies), and hovering a target (unit or tile) shows the spell's hovered icon as the cursor (`GetHoveredIcon`, falling back to `Icon`; `ShowSpellCursor`, a `Sprite2D` scaled to `SpellCursorSize`, 128 px). Clicking one calls `TrySubmitSpell`. A unit spell becomes the turn's action (a `TurnOutput` entry; mana is spent when it runs). A hero spell doesn't end the turn, as in HoMM: `CastHeroSpell` plays it right away (`SpendHeroMana`, then `Arena.PlayHeroSpell`: the hero model's spell cast, and at its `Hit` `SpellAbility.UseFromHero(caster, origin, target, arena)`: `origin` is `GetHeroCastOrigin`, the hero model's `LaunchPoint` or else the global position of `Arena.HeroSpellCastPosition`, a marker where the hero stands off the board, and `caster` the acting stack; by default it calls `Use`, and `FireBallSpell` overrides it to fly from `origin`; `IsPlayerTurn` is false meanwhile, so no input is taken), removes dead stacks, searches `CurrentReachability` again (`RunBattle` executes the turn with it, not the turn-start one) and shows the turn input again; if the battle is over or the stack died, the turn ends. Use Hero's Spell is disabled once the hero has cast this round. `TurnOutput.HeroSpell` is now only for the computer's turns. Right click, Escape (`Arena._Input`) or the Cancel Spell button (`BattleInterface/CancelSpell`, shown only while targeting) cancel back to the normal input (`CancelSpellTargeting`, `ShowTurnTargets`). The player's own units take clicks only as spell targets.

**Stack labels** (`ArenaUserInterface`, `#region Stack labels`): `Arena.SpawnUnitNode` calls `AddStackLabel(stack, playerSide)`, which instantiates `scenes/ui/stack_lable.tscn` (root `StackLabel`, `src/ui/StackLabel.cs`: only the `Quantity` on a `NumberTag` whose image is `PlayerTagTexture` or `ComputerTagTexture` by side, centered on the root's origin; it ignores the mouse, so clicks reach the units and tiles). `Arena.RemoveDeadStacks` calls `RemoveStackLabel`. Every frame the labels refresh their quantity and move to `Arena.GetStackLabelCanvasPoint(stack)`: the footprint's near edge on screen (the center's projected X, the lowest projected corner's Y), so a 1x1 stack's tag sits at its feet and a 2x2 one's in front of the model rather than over it, from any camera angle, from the unit's current position, so it follows moves and the setup drag. Projection goes through `Arena.ProjectToCanvas` (the camera's `UnprojectPosition`, then `ViewportRect.ViewportToCanvas`; the sword cursor uses it too). `StackLabel.PlaceAt` centers the tag on that point, plus `ScreenOffset`. Under the tag, `StackLabel.ShowPreview(killed, revived)` shows what the hovered action would do: `-N` units killed (`KillPreviewColor`) or `+N` revived (`RevivePreviewColor`), nothing for damage that kills no unit or for heals; the `Preview` label is made in code. `Arena.UpdateActionPreview` (every frame, player turns) computes it from the abilities' own amounts: the picked melee attack's or hovered ranged target's `GetDamage` through `Stack.GetUnitsKilledBy`, or the picked spell's `Evaluate`/`EvaluateFromHero` (`UnitsKilled`, `Revives`); retaliation isn't included. `ArenaUserInterface.SetActionPreview(stack, killed, revived)` shows it on one stack and clears the previous one. They sit in a `StackLabels` control, hidden while the spellbook is open. Draw order is by `ZIndex` (`ArenaUserInterface.SetDrawOrder`), not child order: labels at 0, then the interfaces, the stack tooltip and the cursors on top.

**Tooltips:** two kinds, each a scene with a script; their labels are found by unique name (`%Name`), so the layout can change freely.

- `StackTooltip` (`scenes/ui/stack_tooltip.tscn`): Arena calls `ArenaUserInterface.SetHoveredStack(GetFocusedStack())` every frame. After the mouse rests on a stack for `StackTooltipDelay`, the tooltip shows next to the mouse (`StackTooltipOffset`, flipped to stay on screen by `KeepOnScreen`) and refreshes every frame. It's hidden while the spellbook is open and ignores the mouse. It shows `Unit.Name` and `Description`, quantity, top health, mana (if the unit has any), Strength/Defense/Speed as the unit's base value plus or minus what effects and the hero add together (`7 + 3`), whether it can melee and shoot, and how many spells and active effects it has. Abilities and effects have no icons or descriptions yet, so they're counts.
- `SpellTooltip` (`scenes/ui/spell_tooltip.tscn`): Godot's built-in tooltip on the spellbook's `SpellButton`s, so it uses Godot's tooltip delay. `SpellButton._MakeCustomTooltip` returns `SpellTooltip.Create(spell, IsHeroSpell, CantCastReason)`: icon, name, mana cost, Knowledge needed (hero spells only), targets, `SpellAbility.Description`, and in red why it can't be cast now (`ArenaUserInterface.GetCantCastReason`). `TooltipText` is set to the spell's name only because Godot shows no tooltip for empty text.

**UI:** `src/ui/custom_controls/PieBar.cs` is a `[GlobalClass][Tool]` `Range` control that draws a radial fill, so it runs in the editor too.

### Battle system (`src/battle_system/`, `src/arena/`)

HoMM3-style turn-based battles on a square grid. Two layers, and the split matters:

- **`Battle`** (`Battle.cs`, plain C#, no nodes) owns state and rules: the round queue, board occupancy, every query (reachability, melee/ranged targets, retaliation, spells) and mutation (`TryPlaceStack`, mana, `RemoveDeadStacks`). Player input and AI both go through it.
- **`Arena`** (`src/arena/Arena.cs`, `Node3D`) owns visuals and execution. It asks `Battle` and displays the answers; it never re-implements rules.

**Data** (`army/`):

- `Unit` (`Resource`): read-only stat sheet shared through `.tres` files. Never mutate it at runtime. `Size` is 1 (one tile) or 2 (2x2). `Model` is the unit's scene; its root must be a `UnitNode`, which Godot can't type-check, so check it when spawning.
- `Stack` (plain class): runtime state per stack: `Quantity`, `TopHealth`, `Mana`, `Effects`, `Anchor`, `Retaliated`, `NodeRepresentation`. `StartingQuantity` is the quantity it was made with (stacks are made per battle), the cap for `Revive` (necromancy: heals the top unit, then brings dead units back; `GetRevivableHealth`, `GetUnitsRevivedBy`). Stat getters like `GetDefense(includeHero)` apply effect modifiers and the hero bonus (`Hero.ApplyBonus`, +10% per point, TBD).
- `Army`: optional `Hero` plus up to 5 `Stacks`. `AddStack` sets `Stack.Army`.
- `Hero` (plain class): Mana, Strength, Knowledge, Defense; `Spells` is a `List<SpellAbility>`. Spell availability is computed on every call (Knowledge >= `MinKnowledge`), so artifact changes apply immediately. One hero cast per round.
- `UnitNode` (`Area3D`, `IInteractable`): root of each unit's model scene, on collision layer 4 so the camera ray hits it. In `_Ready` it copies every surface's material (the surface override, else the `Mesh` resource's own, which is where imported `.glb` models keep them) into a duplicated surface override, so the outline (`StandardMaterial3D` stencil outline) and ghost effects, which only touch the overrides, apply per unit. Two outlines share the stencil: the hover one (`OutlineModel`, white for the player, red for the computer) and the active one (`IsActive`, `ActiveOutlineColor`/`ActiveOutlineThickness`, gold by default) on the unit whose turn it is, for both sides; hover wins while both apply. The `Arena.CurrentStack` setter moves `IsActive` from the previous stack's unit to the new one. Subscribe to a signal *before* triggering the action, and make sure freed nodes don't leave an await hanging.
- Unit animations: an animated model has a `UnitAnimationManager` (`src/animation_mangers/`, e.g. `scenes/units/warrior_animation_manager.tscn`, instanced in `warrior_quendi.tscn`) with bool flags (`IsRunning`, `MeleeAttack`, `RangedAttack`, `SpellCast`) that its `AnimationTree`'s state machine reads in its transitions' advance expressions (`advance_expression_base_node` is the manager). The manager plays them: `MovementStyle.Walk` holds `IsRunning` (set by `UnitNode`) while `MoveAlong` moves; `Teleport` plays nothing (the value is 2 in scenes; Dash was removed). `UnitAnimationManager.PlayMeleeAttack`/`PlayRangedAttack`/`PlaySpellCast` (`PlayOneShot`) return a `UnitAnimation` (top-level record struct, in `UnitAnimationManager.cs`) of two tasks; `UnitNode`'s methods of the same names call them, or return `UnitAnimation.None` without a manager. Heroes' models use the same manager (see Heroes below). They hold the flag until the state machine enters a non-looping state, then watch until it leaves it. `Hit` becomes `true` when the animation calls `UnitAnimationManager.EmitHit` (a Call Method track on the manager, keyed at the frame the weapon connects, the arrow leaves or the spell is released), or at the animation's end if it has none; it's `false` after 0.3 s if no such state starts (no manager, or no state for that flag). `Finished` completes when the state is left, giving up `AnimationEndMargin` after the animation's length. Both always complete, even if the node is freed. So each one-shot state needs a transition back at its end (switch mode At End). The abilities await these: the melee attack deals its damage at `Hit` (or does the old lunge if it's false), the ranged attack and unit spells launch their projectile or effect at `Hit`, and each waits for `Finished` before the turn goes on. Hero casts play no animation on the acting stack (`HealSpell.UseFromHero`).
- Heroes (`Arena`, `#region Heroes`): the hero doesn't fight, but it has a model standing off the board. `Arena.PlayerHeroModel`/`ComputerHeroModel` are scenes whose root is a `UnitAnimationManager` (Oromë: `scenes/orome/orome_animation_manager.tscn`, set on `arena.tscn`); `SpawnHeroModels` (after the player's units in the placement phase, and in `StartBattle`) puts one at `HeroSpellCastPosition` for each army with a `Hero` (the computer's mirrored), facing the other side like the units. `PlayHeroSpell` plays its spell cast for every hero spell and releases the spell at its `Hit`, from its `UnitAnimationManager.LaunchPoint` if set. Without a model, nothing changes: the spell plays right away from the marker.
- Bone constraints (`src/animation_system/constraints/`, `[Tool]`, applied on `Skeleton3D.SkeletonUpdated` so they follow animations, in the editor too) stand in for Blender object constraints that glTF doesn't export, for props parented to bones: `FlowTransformAndDampToConstraint` (Copy Transforms + Damped Track, the warrior's sword) and `CopyLocationRotationConstraint` (Copy Location with Head/Tail, Offset and Invert + Copy Rotation with Mix: Replace, both World Space, per-axis and with influence; e.g. the wizard's hat). Their axes are Godot's: Blender X, Y, Z are Godot X, -Z, Y, and Blender's default XYZ Euler order is `EulerOrder.Xzy`.

**Abilities and effects** (`abilities/`, `effects/`), all abstract `[GlobalClass]` Resources; concrete ones are subclasses:

- `IAbility.Use(user, target, arena)` returns a `Task`. Bases: `MeleeAttackAbility`, `LongRangeAttackAbility` (both with abstract `GetDamage(user, target)`, the damage `Use` deals, for previews and the AI), `SpellAbility` (`ManaCost`, `MinKnowledge`; used for both unit and hero spells), `PassiveAbility` → `RoundPassiveAbility` / `TurnPassiveAbility` (used at the end of each round or of the stack's turn), or a direct `PassiveAbility` subclass for an always-on rule that is checked rather than used: `RangedImmunityPassive` (`Stack.IsImmuneToRanged`; `Battle.GetRangedTargets` leaves the stack out, so nothing can shoot it). Melee attacks: `BasicMeleeAttackAbility` (what it does when it connects is the virtual `Hit`) and `SlowingMeleeAttackAbility`, which also slows (exported `Effect`, `SpeedTierLoss`, `Turns`; the spiders' bite, retaliations too). Every `Use` is `async`. It awaits its animation first and changes game state (damage, heal) after it, so the stack labels change when the hit lands; it changes the state anyway when there's no model or the animation fails to spawn. The state is changed before `Use` returns, which `Arena.ExecuteAbility` relies on (retaliation checks that the target is alive).
- `Projectile` (`projectiles/`, `Node3D`): `Projectile.Spawn(parent, scene, start, end, angle)` instantiates a scene whose root must be a `Projectile` and flies it along a parabola from `start` to `end` (global positions), leaving at `angle` degrees above the XZ plane (clamped to 0..85), at the exported `HorizontalSpeed`. It emits `Landed` on arrival (it doesn't free itself). `HeadDirection` is the current flight direction, and every frame the projectile turns its -Z along it (`LookAt`, scale kept), so a projectile scene's model must have its head toward -Z; the root's own rotation is overwritten. Unit abilities launch from `UnitNode.GetLaunchPosition(ShootHeight)`: the unit's exported `LaunchPoint` (e.g. a `Marker3D` under a `BoneAttachment3D` on the hand, read at the hit frame) if set, else its model's `UnitAnimationManager.LaunchPoint`, else `ShootHeight` above the unit; `ShootHeight` is also the height they hit the target at.
- `AbilityVisualEffect` (`visual_effects/`, `[GlobalClass]` `Node3D`): `AbilityVisualEffect.Spawn(parent, scene, position)` instantiates a scene whose root must be one, moves it to `position` (global), and emits `Finished` after the exported `Duration`, or never if `Endless` (it plays until freed, as effects shown on stacks do). It doesn't free itself.
- Spells (`abilities/specific/`): `FireBallSpell` deals `Damage` and flies its exported `Projectile` scene from the caster to the target (what it does on landing is the virtual `Hit`); `WebBallSpell` (`web_ball_spell.tres`, the `ball_of_web.tscn` projectile) is a `FireBallSpell` that also slows a target that survives: it adds its exported `Effect` (a `SlowEffect`) and sets the stack's copy to its own `SpeedTierLoss` and `Turns`; `HealSpell` heals `Amount` (`Stack.Heal`: top unit only, never revives) and plays its exported `VisualEffect` scene on the target; `NecromancySpell` (`necromancy_spell.tres`, "Raise the Fallen", allies) plays its `VisualEffect` and then `Stack.Revive`s the target by `Amount` for a hero's cast, or `AmountPerCaster` times the caster's quantity for a unit's (the Forest Ghost's). Slowing goes through `SlowEffect.Inflict(target, template, loss, turns)`; all slows share `slow_effect.tres` as their `Source`, so a new slow from any of them replaces the stack's current one. Both export a `PackedScene` because Resources can't export nodes. Every `SpellAbility` has `Targets` (`TargetSide`: `Enemies`, `Allies` or `Any`, from the caster's side), and `Battle.GetSpellTargets(stack, spell)` gives the living stacks it can be cast on.
- `SpellAbility.Evaluate(caster, target, battle)` (and `EvaluateFromHero`, defaulting to it) says what a cast would do without doing it, for the AI: a `SpellEstimate` (`abilities/SpellEstimate.cs`) of facts, not a score: `Damage`/`PotentialDamage`, `UnitsKilled`, `Healing`/`PotentialHealing`, `Revives`/`PotentialRevives` (necromancy). The real values are capped like the mutations are (`ForDamage` through the units up to total health via `Stack.GetUnitsKilledBy`, `ForHealing` up to the top unit's missing health, `ForRevive` up to `Stack.GetRevivableHealth`), so `Overkill`/`Overheal` are the waste; `KillsStack(target)` is a whole-stack kill. Each spell computes its amount in one private method that both `Use` and `Evaluate` call (`FireBallSpell.GetDamage`, `HealSpell.GetHealing`), so the AI's estimate can't drift from the real cast. Add fields to the struct for new kinds of spells rather than scoring inside spells; strategies do the weighing.
- `IEffect.Apply(target, arena)`: `RoundEffect` / `TurnEffect` with stat modifiers and `Duration`. `Stack.AddEffect` duplicates the Resource, because runtime state must never live on a shared `.tres`, and keeps the original as the copy's `Source`: adding an effect that's already on the stack only resets its `RemainingDuration`, so the same effect never stacks. An effect's exported `VisualEffect` (an `Endless` `AbilityVisualEffect` scene) plays on the unit while it lasts: `AddEffect` calls `UnitNode.ShowEffect` (spawned as the unit's child at its origin, so it follows the unit and goes when the unit is freed) and `RemoveExpiredEffects` calls `HideEffect`. `Stack.GetSpeedTier` runs the base speed through each effect's `ModifySpeedTier` (by default `+ SpeedTierModifier`), so an effect can cap itself. The only concrete effect is `SlowEffect` (`TurnEffect`, so `Duration` counts the slowed stack's turns; `assets/battle_system/effects/slow_effect.tres`: lowers speed by `-SpeedTierModifier`, never below `MinSpeedTier` 3 or lowering a stack already at or below it; visual `scenes/visual_effects/slowness_effect.tscn`).

**Board:**

- Grid of `Arena.AreaWidth` x `Arena.AreaHeight` (static, 20x12), tiles `Arena.TileSize` (0.5 m), centered on the Arena's origin. Convert grid to Arena-local positions only with `Arena.GridToWorld` (tile center, any grid point, or a stack's footprint center); the tile generation uses it too, so tiles and units stay aligned. `Stack.Anchor` is the top-left tile of the stack's footprint; the other tiles come from `Size`. Occupancy maps every covered tile to the same `Stack`, so `GetStackAt` works on any of them, and turns stay one per stack.
- `Battle.GetReachability(stack)` is a breadth-first search over anchors: 4 directions (no diagonal steps), up to `SpeedTier` steps, and a step is allowed only if the whole footprint `Fits` (in bounds, not on another stack; own tiles count as free). That stops 2x2 stacks squeezing through 1-wide gaps. It returns a `Reachability`: reachable anchors with where each was first reached from, so `GetPath(anchor)` gives a shortest path without searching again. `GetTiles()` gives tiles to highlight, `GetAnchorsCovering(tile)` the valid 2x2 squares under the mouse.
- Search once per turn: `Arena` stores it as `CurrentReachability` and passes it to the queries' optional `reachability` parameter. It's stale once the board changes.
- Adjacency compares whole footprints (`AreAdjacent(Stack, Stack)`).

**Turn flow** (`Arena.RunBattle`):

1. Before the battle: `SceneTransitions.EnterArena(tree, player, computer)` (`src/helpers/`) creates the arena and calls `SetArmies`. `BeginBattle()` creates the `Battle` after the placement phase (below), with `keepAnchors` so the stacks stay where they were placed. Without it (`AutoBeginBattle`, which `_Ready` currently doesn't start), the `Battle` constructor auto-places stacks (`PlaceArmy`) at the anchors from the static `Battle.GetStartingAnchors(stacks, leftSide, width, height)`, whose result is index-aligned with `stacks`.
2. Round queue order: `SpeedTier` descending, then Defense ascending (effects included, hero excluded), then `Quantity` ascending. Rebuilt every round.
3. Each turn waits one frame first (otherwise instantly finished turns freeze the game). Player stacks wait on a `TaskCompletionSource` completed by `SubmitPlayerTurn(TurnOutput)`; computer stacks ask the AI (below).
4. `TurnOutput`: `HeroSpell` (the computer's only) runs first, like the player's hero casting before their stack acts, then `MoveTo` (an anchor), then `Entries` grouped by wave. Entries in one wave run in parallel (`Task.WhenAll`); waves run in order. `TurnOutput.Add(ability, target)` sets the wave to the number of earlier entries on the same target.
5. After a melee hit, the target retaliates in the next wave if `Battle.CanRetaliate` (`Unit.CanRetaliate`, not `Stack.Retaliated` this round, alive, has melee). Retaliations don't trigger retaliations.
6. Wait (the `Wait` button, `Arena.TryWait`): a `TurnOutput` with `Wait` set. `Battle.Wait` puts the stack at the end of this round's queue and sets `Stack.Waited` (once per round, reset with `Retaliated`); the turn doesn't end, so step 7 runs only after the turn it really takes. Waited stacks act in the order they waited (HoMM3 reverses it; TBD). `ArenaUserInterface.SetWaitEnabled` enables the button only on a player turn whose stack can wait.
7. End of turn: turn passives, turn effects, expired effects removed, dead stacks removed (`Arena.RemoveDeadStacks`: `Battle.RemoveDeadStacks` takes them out of the armies, round queue and board, then their `UnitNode`s are freed). End of round: round passives and effects, then `StartNewRound` resets `Retaliated` and the hero's cast.

**Placement phase** (`Arena.SetupPlacementPhase`, used when `AutoBeginBattle` is off): spawns the player's `UnitNode`s at `GetStartingAnchors` and enables the first `Arena.PlacementColumns` (2) columns of tiles. The player drags stacks: pressing on a unit locks the interaction, the stack follows the focused tile, and releasing drops it. `Battle` doesn't exist yet, so `Arena.CanPlaceDuringSetup` checks placement instead of `Battle.Fits`, and `Stack.Anchor` follows the model. `FindSetupAnchorCovering` moves a 2x2 stack as little as needed to cover the hovered tile. A 2x2 stack stands on a `DoubleTile` that is a child of its `UnitNode` (`GroundTile`), and stands in for the 4 small tiles under it: `Arena.ShowTiles`/`HideTiles`/`HideAllTiles` keep those small tiles disabled and show or hide the double tile in their place (by the stacks' current anchors), in the battle too. So a double tile shows only when the tiles it covers would. A double tile under a unit takes its `GridPosition` from the stack's current anchor (like `UnitNode.GridPosition`), so hovering or clicking it finds the stack wherever it has moved; only `MovePreview` stores its own. The Start Battle button (`ArenaUserInterface`) emits `SetupPlacementPhaseCompleted`, then `TransitionToBattle` spawns the computer's units, calls `UnitNode.SwitchToBattleInteractions` on every unit (swaps the setup drag callbacks for the battle ones of its side, picked by `IsPlayerUnit`, all still empty; focus/outline is shared), hides the tiles and moves the camera to `BoardOverviewCameraPosition` (`ArenaCamera.MoveTo`). Then `BeginBattle`. `SpawnUnitNode` sets `Stack.NodeRepresentation` for both sides.

**Melee input** (player turns, all in `Arena`): at turn start the enemies in `Battle.GetMeleeTargets` get their tiles shown red (`SetTilesHighlight`; `IsMeleeAttackTile` swaps in the shared red materials in `assets/battle_system/tiles/`). Every frame, `UpdateTurnHover` takes the focused melee target (its unit or the tile under it) and `PickMeleeAttackAnchor` picks, from `GetMeleeAttackAnchors`, the anchor whose direction from the target's footprint center is closest to the direction to `ArenaCamera.GetBoardHoverPoint()` (the mouse ray on the board plane, so tall models don't skew it); that footprint is red and `Raised`. Clicking the unit or its tile calls `TrySubmitMeleeAttack` (move to the picked anchor, then melee). While a pick exists, `UpdateMeleeCursor` hides the mouse in place and shows the sword (`ArenaUserInterface.ShowMeleeCursor`): the picked footprint's center and a point 1 m further toward the target are projected with `PlayerCamera.UnprojectPosition` and mapped to the screen by `ViewportRect.ViewportToCanvas` (the camera renders into a SubViewport); the sword sits at the first point, rotated from screen-up (`Vector2.Up`, Y points down) to the second. On a 2x2 stack's turn, `Arena.MovePreview` (a second `DoubleTile`, Arena's own) shows the destination: hovering a reachable small tile moves it to the closest anchor covering that tile from where it is (`ClosestAnchor`, shared with the setup drag), hovering the stack's own double tile resets it, and a melee pick moves it to the attack anchor. It's hidden while on the stack's own position, and stands in for the small tiles under it via `GetDoubleTilesByTile`. Clicking it or a small tile calls `TrySubmitMoveTo`, which uses the preview's anchor for 2x2 stacks. `UnitNode.MoveAlong` tweens `position` to footprint centers, and `FaceTowards` keeps the double tile square after turning the unit. Melee comes before ranged: an enemy the stack can reach in melee isn't in `GetRangedTargets`, as in HoMM 5. The enemies in `GetRangedTargets` get red tiles too; hovering one (unit or tile) shows the arrow cursor (`ArenaUserInterface.ShowRangedCursor`: a `Sprite2D` that follows the mouse with the arrowhead's tip, `RangedCursorHotspot`, on it, while the mouse is hidden. Not a custom OS cursor: `Input.SetCustomMouseCursor` fails in the editor's embedded game view on macOS in 4.7.2 with "Parameter imgrep is null"), and clicking calls `TrySubmitRangedAttack` (shoot from where the stack stands). `BasicLongRangeAttackAbility` flies a `Projectile` (`scenes/projectiles/basic_projectile.tscn`) to the target and awaits `Landed`. Everything is cleared after the turn.

**Cinematic camera** (`Arena`, `#region Cinematic camera`; on with `Arena.CinematicCamera`): the models are small at the retro resolution, so each turn is shown in close shots. `RunBattle` wraps `ExecuteTurn` in `BeginCinematic`/`EndCinematic` (skipped for turns that do nothing, and for Wait), and `CastHeroSpell` wraps the player's hero spell. Begin turns input off (`PlayerCamera.InteractionEnabled`: nothing is hovered or clicked, so tooltips and outlines go away too), points the camera at the acting unit and waits `CinematicLeadIn`; End sends the camera back to `BoardOverviewCameraPosition` (`ArenaCamera.MoveTo`, `CinematicReturnDuration`) and turns input back on. The shot is `ArenaCamera.SetShotFocus(func)`: every frame the camera eases (`ShotFollowSpeed`) toward looking at the point from `ShotDistance` away, pitched `ShotPitch` down, with the heading it had when the shots began; `null` stops it. The point is `Arena.FollowNodes(...)`: the middle of the nodes, `CinematicFocusHeight` up, kept where a node was last seen once it's freed. So the move follows the walking unit. Waves run in parallel, but the camera shows one ability per wave (`FocusCinematicOnWave`): the turn stack's own, else the wave's first (a retaliation), framed between attacker and target for melee, else on the user. Hero spell shots start on the hero (`HeroFocus`: its model, or without one its cast point `GetHeroCastOrigin`, mirrored for the computer): a computer turn with a `HeroSpell` begins there, casts, then goes straight to the acting stack (`FocusCinematicOnStack`, another `CinematicLeadIn`) without returning to the overview; the player's mid-turn hero spell begins there too. The stack shown is `_cinematicSubject`; abilities call `Arena.FollowWithCamera(user, node)` for the projectile or effect they spawn, and the camera switches to it only if `user` is that stack (hero spells pass the acting stack). New abilities that spawn something should call it too.

**Testing:** run `scenes/battle_test.tscn` (F6). `src/test/BattleTest.cs` builds the two armies from its exported `StackResource` arrays and enters the arena.

**Damage:** attacks use `Battle.CalculateDamage(attacker, target, multiplier)`: `multiplier × Strength × quantity − Defense × target quantity / 10`, never negative, with effects and the hero bonus in both stats (TBD, to be balanced). `BasicMeleeAttackAbility` and `BasicLongRangeAttackAbility` pass their `BasicAttackMultiplier`. Spells ignore Defense by design (their speciality), so they don't use it: `FireBallSpell` deals its `Damage` times the caster's quantity for unit casts, and `Damage` alone for hero casts.

**AI** (`src/battle_system/ai/`, plain C# like `Battle`): the computer army's `Army.Strategy` (`EnemyStrategy`; `FightPOI.ComputerStrategy` on the map, `BattleTest.ComputerStrategy` in the test) picks a `BattleAI` subclass via `BattleAI.Create`, made in `Arena.StartBattle` and kept for the whole battle, so strategies remember things between turns. `Arena.GetComputerTurn` calls `DecideTurn(stack, reachability)`, which only asks `Battle` and returns a `TurnOutput`, as the player's clicks do. `Arena.ExecuteAbility` calls `OnStackAttacked(attacker, target)` after every attack or unit spell except retaliations; a computer stack hit by a player stack takes it as its target (`BattleAI.Targets`, per stack only, not the whole army). Shared helpers: `Attack` (shoot if it can, else melee from the attack anchor with the shortest walk), `MoveToward` (to the reachable anchor with the fewest steps left to the target), `StepsTo`/`TurnsTo`, `GetNearestEnemy`, all built on `Battle.GetStepsToAttack(stack, target)`: an unlimited breadth-first search outward from the anchors next to the target, around other stacks, so distances follow real paths. Strategies:

`BattleAI.DecideTurn` is the same for every strategy: the subclass's `DecideStackTurn` (cast, attack or move), then a hero spell in `TurnOutput.HeroSpell` if the hero can cast and `ShouldCastHeroSpell` says now. Casting goes through `GetCastOptions` (every spell on every valid target with its `SpellEstimate`) and the subclass's `ChooseCast`, the same for the stack's own spells and the hero's. A caster casts whenever `ChooseCast` finds something, without moving; out of mana it falls back to attacking, and a stack with no attack stays put while it has castable spells, then wanders randomly (`IdleTurn`). The computer's hero casts from `Arena.GetHeroCastOrigin`: the `HeroSpellCastPosition` marker mirrored to the computer's side.

- `WildAI` (`Wild`): attacks its target if it can this turn, else the nearest enemy it can attack, else walks toward its target. The first target is a random enemy, a dead one is replaced by the nearest enemy. Casting, all at random: any revive; else a heal that doesn't overheal an ally (random among those pairs); else a damage spell on its own target. The hero casts on a random turn of the army's each round (chance 1 / turns left, so always by the last).
- `GreedyAI` (`GreedyWeak`/`GreedyStrong`): ranks enemies by kill fraction (`BattleAI.GetAttackDamage` against total health) or by threat (`GetAttackDamage` the enemy would deal to this stack); `GetAttackDamage` is the stronger of the stack's attacks' own `GetDamage`, so ability multipliers count. A stack that can shoot attacks the best target it can hit; a melee-only one picks the lowest rank + turns of walking, or heads for the top enemy if none can be reached. Focus fire comes for free, since damage lands before the next stack decides. An attacked stack chases its attacker until it dies. Casting: revives the slowest ally (least wasted revive); else heals the top ally of its ally ranking (weak: most missing health; strong: hits the enemy hardest) once it's missing at least `HealThreshold` (25%) of its top unit, with the least overheal; else hits its target (the attacker or the top enemy) with the stack-killing cast of least overkill, or the most damage if nothing kills. The hero casts on the army's last turn each round.
- An `Organized` strategy (melee stacks guarding the ranged ones, groups moving together, using Wait) was designed but dropped for time; no AI uses Wait.
- `Arena.ComputerTurnDelay` (0.4 s): a computer stack waits that long, already outlined as active, before `DecideTurn`, so the player can follow the computer's turns.
- `BattleTest.ComputerHeroSpells`: gives the computer a hero (same stats as the player's) when it has spells, to test hero casting.

**Not built yet:** hit or death animations, effects besides `SlowEffect`, turn or round passives (the only passive is `RangedImmunityPassive`; the concrete abilities are `BasicMeleeAttackAbility`, `SlowingMeleeAttackAbility`, `BasicLongRangeAttackAbility`, `FireBallSpell`, `WebBallSpell`, `HealSpell` and `NecromancySpell`), surrender (the Forest Ghost is meant to surrender and teach the hero `NecromancySpell`), and returning to the adventure map after a battle (`Arena.BattleEnded` fires, but nothing listens).

## Conventions

- **Keep this file current.** When a change makes anything in this file out of date (architecture, battle system, design decisions), update this file in the same change.

- Nullable reference types are on: node and resource fields filled in `_Ready` or by exports are declared nullable (`Label?`) and dereferenced with `!` or checked.
- Godot C# scripts are `public partial class` with the same name as the file. Each `.cs` file has a committed `.cs.uid` sidecar. Scenes reference scripts and resources by `uid://`, so keep uid files when moving or renaming files.
- `Battle`, `Arena` and `UnitNode` are split into `#region`s; put new members in the matching region (or add one).
- Tunables are exposed via `[Export]` (grouped with `[ExportGroup]`/`[ExportSubgroup]`) rather than hardcoded.
- Prefer editing `.tscn` and `project.godot` through the Godot editor. If you edit them by hand, preserve `uid`/`unique_id` values.
- Claude: change files with the Edit/Write tools, never with Python, `sed` or other scripts. The user reviews every change as a diff in the IDE, and script edits don't show up there.
